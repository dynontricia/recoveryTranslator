Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 25, 2026

Recovery Translator was built by and for the recovery community, where anonymity matters. This page explains, in plain language, what happens to what is said in a meeting while Recovery Translator is running, and what little we keep.

The short version

  • No personal accounts, no names, no cookies, no ads, no analytics.
  • We do not record or save meeting audio, captions, or translations.
  • Meeting audio is sent to Google's Gemini API to be translated into the languages the leader chooses.
  • With our key, or a group key from a Google project with billing turned on, Google's paid terms apply: Google does not use the audio to train or improve its products.
  • Google keeps what is sent and returned for up to 55 days, only to detect abuse of its service, such as hate speech or harassment. If its automated systems flag something, authorized Google staff may review it.
  • If a group saves its own Gemini key, we keep that key encrypted until the group removes it or removes the Zoom app.
  • Anything said aloud while captions are running is sent for translation, so share with that in mind.

What we never collect

Recovery Translator does not ask for anyone's name, email, phone number, or any other personal details. Listeners join with only a session code. Leaders use an access code or their group's saved key, neither of which is tied to a person's identity.

What happens to meeting audio

While a session is running, audio from the leader's microphone (or from the Zoom meeting, through the Recovery Translator Zoom app) passes through our server straight to Google's Gemini API. Google returns captions and spoken translation in the session's languages, and our server passes them straight on to the people in the session. Nothing is written to disk along the way, and nothing remains on our server after the session ends.

How Google handles it

Every session uses one Gemini API key: ours, or one a group has saved. Which Google terms apply depends on that key.

Paid tier (our key, and any group key from a Google project with billing turned on):

  • Google does not use the audio, captions, or translations to train or improve its products.
  • Google processes the data as a data processor, under its Data Processing Addendum.
  • Google logs what is sent and what comes back for up to 55 days. It uses these logs only to detect and prevent violations of its Prohibited Use Policy, and for any legally required disclosures. It does not use them to train AI models, other than the systems that enforce that policy.
  • Google's automated systems scan for the kinds of misuse its Prohibited Use Policy lists. For example:
    • Hate speech or harassment: using the service to attack, threaten, or bully people.
    • Sexually explicit or dangerous content: using it to produce explicit material, or instructions that could cause serious harm.
    These rules target misuse of the service. They are not aimed at people sharing honestly about their own experience. Flagged content may be reviewed by authorized Google employees.
  • The data may be stored briefly or cached in any country where Google or its agents have facilities.

Free tier (a group key from a project without billing): under Google's terms for unpaid use, Google may use what is sent to improve its products, and people at Google may read it. Groups should only save a key from a project with billing turned on. The documentation shows how. We can't see which tier a group's key is on.

Google does not receive anyone's name or account from us; it receives the audio itself. But a voice, or details someone shares, could still identify them.

Protecting anonymity in your meeting

  • Leaders: please let the group know at the start that the meeting is being translated live by an AI service, and briefly explain what that means.
  • Members: as you would in any open meeting, consider using first names only and leaving out details that could identify you or others, such as last names, workplaces, or places.
  • Everything said aloud while captions are running is sent for translation. In Zoom, the leader can tap Pause to stop sending audio for part of a meeting, or end the session.

What our server keeps

  • During a session: the session code, its languages, which screens are connected, and the Gemini key it uses. This is held in memory only and deleted when the session ends.
  • Saved group settings: when a group saves its own Gemini key in the Zoom app, or we set up an access code for a group, we keep a record with the group name it chose, the key encrypted (and its last four characters, so the group can recognize it), the access code as a one-way hash, a one-way hash of the Zoom user ID that saved it, and when it was created. We never store the access code itself, the key in readable form, or the Zoom user ID itself. The record is deleted when the group taps Remove saved key, removes the Zoom app, or asks us to delete it.
  • Technical logs: our hosting provider keeps routine logs, such as session codes, connection and error events, Zoom stream IDs, and a short one-way hash of Zoom meeting IDs. These logs never include audio, captions, translations, keys, or names.
  • Zoom app installation: when the Zoom app is installed, Zoom sends us an authorization token. We use it only to finish the installation, and we don't keep it.

What stays on your device

Some pages remember small settings in your browser, such as caption text size, display colors, and the leader's language choices. The Zoom app also remembers the leader's access code. These stay on your device. The access code is sent to us only when you start a session, to check it. We do not use cookies.

Other services

Like any website, our hosting provider can see the IP address of each visitor; we use it only briefly in memory to slow down repeated wrong access codes. The pages load a QR-code library from Cloudflare's cdnjs, and the Zoom app loads Zoom's own app software. When used inside Zoom, Zoom's privacy policy also applies.

Changes

If how Recovery Translator handles data changes, we will update this page and the date at the top.

Step-by-Step Instructions

Session Leaders

  1. Tap I am a session leader and enter your access code.
  2. Under Languages, check the caption languages your meeting needs: English, Spanish, Polish, or French. Check Audio for any language listeners should also hear read aloud. Each language adds to the translation cost, so pick only what you need.
  3. Choose where the audio comes from — this computer (a microphone, or system audio through BlackHole) or a Zoom meeting — and tap Start session.
  4. For a Zoom meeting, the leader screen shows a Zoom link code. In the meeting, open Apps → Recovery Translator, enter it under Started a session in the browser?, and captions start. (You can also start the whole session from the Zoom app.)
  5. Share the QR code or session code. Optionally open the big display screen for a room projector, or the captions-only pop-out window for a simpler view.
  6. Speak normally, in any language — every caption box updates live.
  7. Tap End session when the meeting is over. Sessions from a Zoom meeting also end on their own when the meeting does.

Listeners

  1. Scan the QR code or tap I am a listener, enter the session code, choose your language from the ones the session offers, and tap Join.
  2. Captions appear automatically. Languages marked 🔊 can also be heard: tap the 🔊 button to listen.
  3. If you switch apps or lock your phone briefly, reopening the page should reconnect you automatically.

About Recovery Translator

Recovery Translator provides real-time spoken language interpretation and live captioning for recovery meetings, area committees, and service assemblies.

How It Works

The leader opens a session, picks its languages (English, Spanish, Polish, or French), and shares a QR code. Whatever language someone speaks, listeners read live captions in the language they choose, and can hear a live spoken translation in the languages the leader turned audio on for.

Getting Access

Leaders need an access code, or their group's own Gemini key saved in the Zoom app's Settings. Listeners only need the session code. To request an access code, contact dynontricia@gmail.com.

Built for Recovery

Built by and for the recovery community, with anonymity and accessibility in mind. Free to use and open source.
