How to add, use, and remove Recovery Translator.

What it does

Recovery Translator gives a meeting live captions in the languages the leader chooses (English, Spanish, Polish, and French), whatever language someone speaks, plus an optional spoken translation people can listen to on their own phone. Listeners need no account or app; they join with a 6-character session code or QR code and pick their language.

Audio can come from a computer's microphone (or its system audio), or from a Zoom meeting through the Recovery Translator Zoom app. Translation is done by Google's Gemini Live API.

Requirements

  • To start sessions, one of:
    • an access code (email dynontricia@gmail.com to request one), or
    • your group's own Gemini API key, from a Google project with billing turned on, saved in the Zoom app's Settings (see below).
    Listeners don't need either.
  • For the Zoom app: a Zoom account that can host meetings, the Zoom desktop client, and Zoom's Realtime Media Streams enabled for your account. You'll be asked to allow the app to access meeting audio when you start captions.
  • Listeners: any phone, tablet, or computer with a current web browser.

Using your group's own Gemini key

Your group can pay Google directly for its own translation instead of using an access code. Google bills your group's Google account.

Turn on billing. With a free-tier key, Google may use your meeting audio to improve its products, and people at Google may read it. That is not safe for an anonymous meeting. Only use a key from a project with billing turned on, so Google's paid terms apply.
  1. Sign in to Google AI Studio (aistudio.google.com) with your group's Google account and open Get API key.
  2. Create a key. Google puts it in a Google Cloud project.
  3. On the same page, choose Set up billing for that project and link a billing account. Google's screens change from time to time; what matters is that the project's plan shows a paid tier, not Free.
  4. In Google Cloud Billing, set a budget alert, so you hear about costs before they surprise you. Each caption language in a session is a separate translation, so four languages cost about twice what two do.
  5. In a Zoom meeting, open Apps → Recovery Translator → Settings, paste the key, add your group's name if you like, and tap Save key. We check the key with Google, then store it encrypted.
  6. The first time, the panel shows a website access code, only once. Write it down: it lets you start sessions on the website with your key.

After that, you start sessions in Zoom without a code. To change the key, paste a new one in Settings. To delete it, tap Remove saved key, or remove the app from Zoom.

Adding the Zoom app

  1. Sign in to the Zoom App Marketplace and open the Recovery Translator listing.
  2. Click Add, review the permissions, and click Allow.
  3. The app now appears under Apps in your Zoom meetings.

Using it in a Zoom meeting

  1. Start your meeting and open Apps → Recovery Translator.
  2. Enter your access code (not needed if your group saved its own key), check the languages the meeting needs, check Audio for any language people should also hear, and tap Start session.
  3. Allow Zoom to share meeting audio with the app when prompted. Captions appear in the panel within a few seconds.
  4. Tap Invite everyone to view captions in Zoom. Zoom asks each participant whether to open Recovery Translator, where they see the captions right in Zoom (see below). Nobody is forced to open it.
  5. Tap Post join link to meeting chat too. The message has a line in each session language, so people on phones, or who'd rather use a browser, can follow along and hear spoken translation.
  6. Use Pause to stop sending audio for part of a meeting, and End session when you're done. The session also ends when the meeting ends.

The panel's text-size buttons (A− / A+) and its language buttons change only your own view. Each session belongs to its own meeting: nobody in another meeting can see it, and only the host or a co-host (or the browser that started it) can pause or end it.

For attendees: captions in Zoom

Attendees don't need an access code, a key, or anything installed in advance. When they open Recovery Translator from the host's invitation, or from Apps, they get a captions-only view:

  • Before the host starts, it says captions will appear when the host starts them, and it switches on by itself.
  • They pick a language from the ones the host chose, and change text size. It starts in their own language if the session has it.
  • They can't start, pause, or end anything. Zoom tells us each person's role in the meeting, and only the host and co-hosts get controls.
  • Spoken translation isn't played inside Zoom, so it doesn't mix with the meeting audio. To listen, use the join link on a phone.

Started the session on the website? Choose A Zoom meeting as the audio source there. The leader screen shows a private Zoom link code. In the meeting's panel, enter it under Started a session in the browser? Each link code works once.

Using it without Zoom

Session leaders

  1. Tap I am a session leader and enter your access code.
  2. Under Languages, check the caption languages your meeting needs: English, Spanish, Polish, or French. Check Audio for any language listeners should also hear read aloud. Each language adds to the translation cost, so pick only what you need.
  3. Choose where the audio comes from — this computer (a microphone, or system audio through BlackHole) or a Zoom meeting — and tap Start session.
  4. For a Zoom meeting, the leader screen shows a Zoom link code. In the meeting, open Apps → Recovery Translator, enter it under Started a session in the browser?, and captions start. (You can also start the whole session from the Zoom app.)
  5. Share the QR code or session code. Optionally open the big display screen for a room projector, or the captions-only pop-out window for a simpler view.
  6. Speak normally, in any language — every caption box updates live.
  7. Tap End session when the meeting is over. Sessions from a Zoom meeting also end on their own when the meeting does.

Listeners

  1. Scan the QR code or tap I am a listener, enter the session code, choose your language from the ones the session offers, and tap Join.
  2. Captions appear automatically. Languages marked 🔊 can also be heard: tap the 🔊 button to listen.
  3. If you switch apps or lock your phone briefly, reopening the page should reconnect you automatically.

Removing the Zoom app

  1. Sign in to the Zoom App Marketplace.
  2. Click Manage → Added Apps.
  3. Find Recovery Translator and click Remove.

When the app is removed, Zoom tells us and we immediately delete any group settings saved from your Zoom account: the encrypted Gemini key and the hashed website access code. See the Privacy Policy.

Data and privacy, in brief

  • No personal accounts or names are collected, and no audio, captions, or translations are saved.
  • Meeting audio is sent over an encrypted connection to Google's Gemini API for translation. On the paid tier, Google does not use it to improve its products, and keeps it for up to 55 days for abuse monitoring only.
  • A group's saved Gemini key is stored encrypted; access codes and Zoom user IDs are stored only as one-way hashes.
  • Everything said while captions are running is sent for translation. Leaders should tell the group at the start of the meeting.
